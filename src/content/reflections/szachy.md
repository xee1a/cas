## Goal
I want to keep getting better at chess and slowly push my rating up. For me it's
a way to train how I think: staying patient, looking a few moves ahead, and not
panicking when a position gets complicated.

## What I do
I play chess **regularly** and solve **puzzles** almost every day. The puzzles
are the part I lean on most, they're quick, I can do a handful whenever I have a
spare few minutes, and they sharpen my tactics: spotting forks, pins, and mating
patterns faster. Alongside that I play full games, and afterwards I look back at
the ones I lost to work out where my plan actually went wrong.

## Reflection
Playing and solving puzzles this consistently has taught me a lot about how I
think under pressure. The biggest lesson has been **patience**: the puzzles that
catch me out are usually the ones where I played the first move that looked good
instead of checking what my opponent could do back. Losing games used to
frustrate me, but treating each loss as something to analyse rather than just a
bad result has made me calmer and more honest about my own mistakes. The habit of
slowing down and thinking a couple of steps ahead is something I notice helping me
outside the board too, when I'm working through a problem that doesn't have an
obvious answer.
